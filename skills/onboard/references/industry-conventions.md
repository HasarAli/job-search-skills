# Industry Conventions — how norms shift by field

Industry modifies the country baseline (see `country-conventions.md`). Country sets the format; industry sets the content emphasis and document type.

| Industry | Document & length | What screens you in | Extras expected | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tech / software | Resume, 1–2 pages | Impact bullets with numbers; stack keywords for ATS/recruiter search; recent, relevant projects | GitHub/portfolio links for ICs; system-scale signals for senior | Skim time is seconds; front-load metrics. Side projects count early-career, fade later. |
| Academia / research | Full CV, no length limit (grows with career) | Publications, citations, grants, teaching record — in that rough order by field | Publication list (full citations), research + teaching statements, referee list up front | The one field where "CV" means everything, everywhere. Chronological completeness expected; gaps are questioned. |
| Healthcare / clinical | CV-style, length flexible | Licenses, certifications, credentials FIRST — they are pass/fail | License numbers, registration status, clinical rotations/placements, immunization/compliance records on request | Credential verification precedes everything; missing license info = instant reject. Country regulator determines required fields. |
| Finance / banking | Resume, strictly 1 page (junior–mid), 2 senior | Prestige signals (institutions, deal names where permitted), quantified P&L/AUM/deal-size numbers, GPA early-career | Deal sheets in some sub-fields; regulatory certifications (Series/CFA etc. per market) | Most conservative formatting: single column, no color, no photo even where country baseline allows. Confidentiality limits deal detail — describe size class, not client names. |
| Government / federal | Long-form detailed application, often 3–6+ pages | Exact match to posted duties and eligibility criteria, addressed point-by-point | Selection-criteria statements (AU), USAJOBS-format detail incl. hours/week and supervisor contacts (US), security-clearance status | The opposite of the private-sector skim resume: completeness beats brevity. Follow the posting's required format exactly — deviation is disqualifying. |
| Creative (design, media, arts) | Short resume + portfolio; portfolio is the real document | Portfolio quality and curation; the resume just has to not disqualify | Portfolio link/PDF, showreel, case studies showing process not just output | Resume design may show taste but must stay readable. Tailor portfolio order to the job — first three pieces decide. |
| Trades / skilled labor | Short resume, 1 page | Tickets/licenses/certifications (trade cert, safety cards, driving licenses), years on the tools | Certification card numbers, union membership where relevant, referee phone numbers | Hiring often phone/referral-speed; make certifications and contactability instantly visible. Formatting polish matters far less than credentials. |

## Cross-cutting notes

- **Keyword matching**: any industry that screens at volume (tech, finance, government, large healthcare systems) filters by keywords first — mirror the posting's exact terms for credentials and skills.
- **Portfolio-adjacent fields** (architecture, UX, journalism, developer relations): treat as creative — the work-sample link outranks bullet polish.
- **Regulated fields** (healthcare, law, finance, aviation, trades): lead with the credential; everything else is secondary. Verify the target country's regulator terminology.
- **Academia → industry switchers**: convert CV to resume (trim publications to a count + top 2–3); industry → academia needs the reverse.
- When country and industry norms conflict (e.g. finance says no photo, DACH baseline says photo), the **industry norm wins inside that industry** — verify locally if unsure.
